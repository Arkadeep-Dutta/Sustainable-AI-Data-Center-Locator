# 06 — Implementation Vision (2026–2056): Western New York AI Campus, Wyoming County

Guiding idea: build the campus as **grid infrastructure, a heat utility, and a community asset**, not only
a load. Every phase is gated on measured performance, in the same spirit as the decision engine.

## Phase 0 — Licence to build (2026 – mid-2027, during the EO 62 state-permit pause)
* Parcel due diligence across the 170 near-best res-7 cells. Prefer disturbed or lower-productivity land.
  Use agricultural-district and zoning screens, wetland delineation, cultural-resource surveys.
* Early government-to-government consultation with the Seneca and Tonawanda Seneca Nations. Public
  hearings, matching the host-community hearing requirement in the pending S10642/A11560 bill even if
  it is not enacted. Engage the Town of Sheldon on its zoning-law revision (draft 22 Jul 2026).
* NYISO large-load interconnection request, phased 100 → 300 MW (option to 600 MW).
* Community Benefits Agreement: local-hire targets, workforce fund with Buffalo/Rochester trades and Genesee
  Community College / Alfred State, and a heat-reuse revenue share. No outsized tax abatement.
* Commission the DEC-style environmental impact statement voluntarily, using this engine's open data and
  methodology.

## Phase 1 — First 100 MW (2028–2031)
* **Cooling:** direct-to-chip liquid (≥ 80 % of heat to liquid at 45–60 °C) + dry coolers with adiabatic
  assist on peak days only. Target annual PUE ≈ 1.09, on-site WUE ≤ 0.05 L/kWh. Use reclaimed or municipal
  greywater for adiabatic water.
* **Power:** two independent 230 kV feeds (0.9 km to existing ≥ 230 kV). On-site substation. Long-lead
  transformers ordered in Phase 0.
* **Clean energy:** 24/7 hourly-matched CFE target ≥ 60 % at energisation from upstate wind/solar PPAs and
  existing nuclear/hydro contracts, with additionality for new supply.
* **Bridging and backup:** grid-scale batteries (≈ 100 MW / 400 MWh) for ride-through and demand
  response. No diesel for routine use; fuel cells capable of RNG/H₂ for extended outages (pilot).
* **Hardware:** OCP Open Rack v3 / ORv3 HPR, OCP modular power shelves, OCP-compliant liquid-cooling
  manifolds. Designed for mid-life refresh without structural rework.

## Phase 2 — Scale to 300 MW and heat-reuse network (2031–2036)
* Expand in 100 MW halls as interconnection upgrades and CFE procurement land.
* **Heat reuse:** a low-temperature hot-water loop with heat pumps lifting to 70–80 °C. Off-takers:
  - controlled-environment agriculture (greenhouses) and aquaculture on adjacent land;
  - dairy and food processing (pasteurisation pre-heat, barn and digester heating);
  - a district-heating pilot for nearby villages, with a 30 km insulated main as a Phase 3 option.
  Target 10–30 % of recovered heat put to use → 21–82 kt CO₂/yr avoided (docs/04).
* CFE target ≥ 90 % hourly-matched by 2035, aligned with NY's 2040 zero-emission grid goal.
* Storage expanded for grid services: the campus becomes a flexible resource for NYISO.

## Phase 3 — Mature operations (2036–2046)
* Hardware generations 3–4: rack-level reuse, component harvesting, certified IT asset disposition,
  ≥ 95 % e-waste diversion, OCP-based refurbishment for secondary markets.
* Grid: participate in capacity and ancillary markets. Seasonal compute shifting to match wind
  availability. Evaluate advanced nuclear/SMR PPAs as NY grid plans mature.
* Water: keep on-site consumption flat as capacity grows (dry-first design). Publish annual audited WUE and
  scarcity-weighted water.

## Phase 4 — Adapt or repurpose (2046–2056)
* Climate adaptation: re-rate dry coolers for the 2050 heat climate. Stormwater upgrades for wetter
  extremes. Review flood mapping every 5 years.
* Shells and power infrastructure are designed for re-use (future compute, storage, or industrial heat
  hubs). Land restoration plan funded from Phase 1.

## Embodied carbon and materials
* Low-clinker concrete (SCMs, ≥ 40 % clinker reduction) and EPD-verified recycled-content steel.
  Track with EC3, with targets set as % below the regional baseline.
* Rail and freight logistics through the Buffalo hub to cut construction transport emissions.
* Prefabricated, OCP-standard modules reduce waste and enable reuse across generations.

## Community benefits
* 24/7 operational jobs plus construction peak. Apprenticeship pipeline.
* Heat-reuse dividend: subsidised heat for farms, greenhouses, and public buildings.
* Annual public sustainability report using this open engine's metrics (carbon: location / market /
  24-7 CFE; water: on-site / off-site / scarcity-weighted; heat reused).
* Ratepayer protection: the campus pays full cost of its interconnection and network upgrades.

## Key performance targets

| Metric | 2030 | 2035 | 2045 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hourly-matched CFE | ≥ 60 % | ≥ 90 % | ≈ 100 % |
| Annual PUE | ≤ 1.10 | ≤ 1.09 | ≤ 1.08 |
| On-site WUE (L/kWh_IT) | ≤ 0.05 | ≤ 0.05 | ≤ 0.05 |
| Recovered heat used | pilot | ≥ 10 % | ≥ 30 % |
| E-waste diverted | ≥ 90 % | ≥ 95 % | ≥ 98 % |
